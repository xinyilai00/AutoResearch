# The Messi Effect: Assessing the Impact of Argentina's 2022 FIFA World Cup Victory on Youth Football Registration Rates

## Abstract

This paper investigates whether Argentina's victory in the 2022 FIFA World Cup on December 18, 2022 — a triumph inextricably linked to the figure of Lionel Messi — produced a measurable increase in youth football registration rates across Argentina. The study adopts an interrupted time series (ITS) framework as its principal analytical design, drawing on methodological precedents from mega-event participation legacy research and sport policy evaluation. The hypothesis posits that the victory generated a statistically significant positive structural break in youth registrations beginning in the first post-victory registration cycle (Q1 2023), with the effect concentrated among the youngest age cohorts (6–12 years), diminishing over subsequent cycles, and more pronounced in provinces with lower baseline registration rates. This pattern would be consistent with an intensification-of-existing-interest mechanism rather than the creation of entirely new participation demand. The analysis draws on a comprehensive review of the sport mega-event legacy literature, athlete role model theory, and South American football culture studies to construct a theoretical framework for the proposed causal pathway. However, the empirical execution of the ITS analysis was not completed due to the unavailability of the required Argentine Football Association (AFA) youth registration data through publicly accessible digital repositories, and the alternative datasets identified during the proposal stage proved inaccessible due to technical constraints. This paper therefore presents the full research design, theoretical scaffolding, and analytical framework for the study, reports the data access limitations transparently, and situates the proposed investigation within the broader literature on sport mega-event participation legacies. The findings underscore both the methodological challenges of conducting participation legacy research in contexts where administrative sports data are not systematically digitized and the critical importance of establishing data infrastructure to capture potential "Messi effect" dynamics in Argentine youth sport.

## Introduction

On December 18, 2022, Argentina defeated France in the FIFA World Cup final in Lusail, Qatar, securing the nation's third World Cup title in a match widely regarded as one of the greatest in the tournament's history. The victory was narratively centered on Lionel Messi, who had by that point accumulated virtually every individual and club honor available in professional football but had not won a senior World Cup with his national team. Across Argentina, the celebrations were unprecedented in scale, with an estimated five million people gathering in Buenos Aires alone for the team's return parade (TODO: verification needed from official government sources). The cultural and emotional significance of this moment extended far beyond sport, intersecting with national identity, collective memory of previous World Cup triumphs in 1978 and 1986, and the socioeconomic anxieties of a nation experiencing persistent inflation and political instability.

The question of whether such a singular sporting moment translates into measurable behavioral change among young people — specifically, increased participation in organized football — is both theoretically significant and practically consequential. The sport mega-event legacy literature has long debated whether hosting or winning major tournaments produces sustained increases in grassroots sport participation, with a growing body of evidence suggesting that such "trickle-down" or "demonstration" effects are frequently overstated (Moussi-Beylie, 2025). Yet the Argentine case presents a potentially unique scenario: football is already the overwhelmingly dominant youth sport in a country where the game is deeply embedded in cultural identity, community life, and social mobility aspirations. In this context, a World Cup victory may operate not by creating new interest in football but by intensifying existing engagement — prompting parents to enroll children earlier, encouraging lapsed participants to return, or motivating clubs to expand their youth programs in anticipation of increased demand.

This paper addresses the following research question: Did Messi's 2022 FIFA World Cup victory produce a measurable increase in youth football registration rates across Argentina, as evidenced by publicly available enrollment data from the Argentine Football Association (AFA), provincial sports ministries, and municipal youth leagues? The hypothesis advanced is that the victory produced a statistically significant positive structural break in registrations beginning in Q1 2023, but that this effect is concentrated in the 6–12 age cohort, diminishes over subsequent registration cycles, and is more pronounced in provinces with lower baseline registration rates — consistent with an intensification-of-existing-interest mechanism.

The study is situated at the intersection of three literatures: the sport mega-event participation legacy field, which provides the methodological template and critical skepticism regarding participation effects; the athlete branding and role model literature, which offers theoretical mechanisms for how individual sporting icons influence youth behavior; and the South American football culture scholarship, which contextualizes the unique institutional and cultural environment in which any "Messi effect" would operate. By integrating these perspectives, the paper aims to contribute both a rigorous analytical framework for studying superstar athlete effects on youth participation and an empirically grounded assessment of whether the 2022 World Cup victory left a detectable imprint on Argentine youth sport.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 reviews the relevant literature across these three domains and identifies the specific gaps this study addresses. Section 3 details the interrupted time series methodology, data requirements, and analytical specifications. Section 4 reports the results of the empirical analysis, including the significant data access constraints encountered. Section 5 discusses the implications of the findings — and the limitations — for theory, policy, and future research. Section 6 concludes with a summary of contributions and a call for institutional data infrastructure development.

## Review

### Sport Mega-Event Participation Legacies

The question of whether major sporting events produce lasting increases in grassroots participation has generated a substantial and increasingly skeptical literature. Moussi-Beylie (2025) conducted a systematic review of the empirical evidence on mega-event sports practice impacts, finding that the promised participation legacies of events such as the Olympic Games are frequently overstated. The review documented modest and short-lived economic benefits, localized job creation, and inconsistent participation effects across multiple host nations and event types. This critical perspective provides an essential counter-narrative to the optimistic claims frequently made by event bidders and government agencies, and it establishes a strong prior expectation that any participation effect from Argentina's 2022 World Cup victory may be transient rather than sustained.

The closest methodological precedent for the present study is the work of 正範 and Hahm (2020), who examined the causal connection between the 2002 FIFA World Cup co-hosted by Japan and South Korea and subsequent football participation and spectatorship in both host nations. Using longitudinal participation data and comparative pre/post event analysis, they found evidence of a causal connection between the mega-event and subsequent football engagement. However, this study examined host-nation effects — where the population was exposed to sustained domestic media saturation, infrastructure investment, and community programming — rather than victory-driven inspiration effects in a nation that won the tournament abroad. The distinction is theoretically important: hosting involves a fundamentally different exposure mechanism than winning, and the causal pathways through which each might influence youth participation are likely distinct.

Chankuna (2025) introduced the "Podium to Profit" framework, which traces the causal pathway from international sports victories to macroeconomic and community-level outcomes, linking sports branding, diplomacy, policy alignment, and entrepreneurship enablement with measurable national income contributions. Using Germany as a reference case and Thailand as the principal case, this framework provides useful theoretical scaffolding for understanding how a sporting triumph might cascade through institutional and social systems to produce behavioral outcomes. However, the framework has not been applied to youth sport participation specifically, nor to the South American context, and its applicability to the Argentine case requires careful adaptation.

### Interrupted Time Series Methods in Sport Policy Evaluation

The interrupted time series (ITS) design has emerged as a rigorous quasi-experimental approach for evaluating the causal impact of discrete policy interventions or events on longitudinal outcome measures. Da (2023) applied ITS regression alongside Random Effects Logistic regression to evaluate the impact of the Homegrown Player Rule on Portuguese Futsal's youth and senior national teams, finding a "relatively" limited impact despite the policy intervention. This study demonstrates both the methodological appropriateness of ITS for detecting structural breaks in sport registration and participation data and the substantive finding that even well-intentioned structural interventions in football often produce modest measurable effects. The application of ITS to the Argentine case follows directly from this precedent, with the 2022 World Cup victory serving as the discrete intervention point in a longitudinal series of youth registration data.

Sdrolias et al. (2026) applied a systems-based multi-criteria framework to evaluate organizational competitiveness in elite professional football, demonstrating how composite indicators can be constructed from heterogeneous institutional data sources. While focused on club-level competitiveness rather than grassroots participation, this work illustrates the methodological challenges of integrating multiple data streams in football research — a challenge directly relevant to the present study's ambition to combine AFA registration data with provincial sports ministry records and municipal league enrollment figures.

### Athlete Branding, Media Construction, and Role Model Effects

The theoretical mechanism through which a World Cup victory might influence youth registration behavior operates partly through the cultural construction and media amplification of the victorious athlete. Hasaan et al. (2019) proposed a framework for understanding the athlete brand life cycle, identifying stages from brand introduction through potential reinvention. This framework provides a useful lens for understanding how Messi accumulated symbolic capital over a career spanning nearly two decades, and how the 2022 World Cup victory may have represented a culminating brand moment with maximal cultural resonance.

Mina and Watson (2024) offered a more directly relevant analysis, examining how Colombian media constructed Linda Caicedo's star status through intersecting narratives of race, gender, and class. Their methodological approach — combining media framing analysis with attention to how star construction operates within specific national contexts — provides a template that could be applied to Messi's representation in Argentine media and its potential effects on young audiences. Wang (2024) further explored how sports celebrity endorsements function as promotional mechanisms, though this work remains oriented toward commercial outcomes rather than psychosocial or developmental impacts on youth.

The media ecosystem surrounding the 2022 World Cup was extensively documented. Rawian et al. (2023) and Avcı and Kilinçarslan (2023) analyzed discourse surrounding the FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022, including the narrative framing of football events and the ways in which the tournament was constructed in public discourse. She et al. (2023) collected at least 130,000 tweets for sentiment analysis of the tournament, while Russo et al. (2024) analyzed approximately two million tweets using multilayer network methods to track topic evolution across tournament stages. These datasets, while not direct measures of youth registration behavior, could serve as supplementary indicators of public engagement and football interest in Argentina following the victory, helping to establish the temporal dynamics of the cultural moment that might have mediated registration decisions.

### South American Football Culture and Youth Development

Understanding the Argentine case requires situating it within the broader context of South American football culture and its distinctive player development pathways. Kolsrud (2018) provided a qualitative study of Uruguayan football culture and its role in producing elite players despite a small population, offering a valuable comparative frame for understanding how footballing culture shapes youth aspiration in the Río de la Plata region. The cultural parallels between Uruguay and Argentina — both in terms of football's centrality to national identity and the institutional structures of youth development — make this comparison particularly instructive.

Ndubuokwu and Oluwalose (2023) examined grassroots soccer development models in Germany and Spain, contrasting structured academy systems with more fragmented approaches. This comparison raises questions about how Argentina's own youth development infrastructure may have been influenced by the visibility of Messi's career trajectory, which itself bridged South American and European development systems. Capon (2012) explored how footballers with migration backgrounds are incorporated into national team narratives and what this means for inclusion and identity — a theme with clear resonance for Messi's own biography as an Argentinian who departed for Barcelona's youth academy as a child and whose relationship with Argentine national identity has been complex and contested throughout his career.

The institutional and political dimensions of Argentine football provide additional context. Orjales (2020) examined the nexus of football, politics, and corruption in Latin America, contextualizing the institutional environment in which Argentine youth football operates. Gift and Miner (2017) argued that political science has systematically understudied the sports-politics nexus, a claim that resonates with the absence of rigorous work on how individual athletes like Messi function as political or cultural symbols within their home nations. Scarfi and Andrade (2022) argued for recentering global history through peripheral nations like Argentina, providing a historiographical framework that situates Argentine cultural exports — including football stars — within broader patterns of global influence.

### Identified Gaps

The literature review reveals a striking absence: no study directly investigates Lionel Messi's impact on Argentinian youth, whether cultural, psychological, aspirational, or developmental. While athlete branding frameworks exist and have been applied to other sporting figures, they have not been applied to Messi specifically or connected to outcomes among youth audiences. The South American football culture literature focuses on institutional and systemic factors rather than the influence of individual iconic athletes. No research has examined whether the 2022 World Cup victory produced measurable shifts in youth football enrollment. And no study has employed longitudinal, survey-based, or ethnographic methods to capture how Argentinian youth themselves articulate the meaning and influence of Messi in their lives. This paper addresses the first of these gaps directly through quantitative analysis of registration data, while acknowledging that the remaining gaps require complementary methodological approaches beyond the scope of the present study.

## Methodology

### Research Design

This study employs a quasi-experimental interrupted time series (ITS) design to assess whether Argentina's 2022 FIFA World Cup victory on December 18, 2022, produced a detectable structural break in youth football registration trends. The ITS design is appropriate because the intervention — a discrete, well-dated sporting event — can be precisely located in time, and the outcome of interest — youth football registrations — is measured at regular intervals before and after the intervention point. This design follows the methodological precedent established by da (2023) in the evaluation of the Homegrown Player Rule in Portuguese Futsal and adapts the participation legacy framework used by 正範 and Hahm (2020) for the 2002 FIFA World Cup.

### Hypothesis Specification

The primary hypothesis (H1) states that Argentina's 2022 World Cup victory produced a statistically significant positive level change in youth football registration rates beginning in Q1 2023, after controlling for pre-existing secular trends, seasonal registration cycles, and demographic shifts. Three subsidiary hypotheses further characterize the expected effect:

H2 (Age specificity): The effect is concentrated in the youngest age cohort (6–12 years), reflecting parental enrollment decisions for young children rather than self-initiated participation by older adolescents.

H3 (Temporal decay): The effect diminishes over subsequent registration cycles, consistent with the mega-event legacy skepticism documented by Moussi-Beylie (2025), manifesting as a significant level change followed by a negative slope change returning toward baseline within two to three quarters.

H4 (Geographic heterogeneity): Provinces with lower pre-victory baseline registration rates exhibit larger relative increases, suggesting the victory activated latent demand in under-participating regions.

### Analytical Model

The baseline ITS regression model takes the following form:

Y_t = β₀ + β₁T_t + β₂D_t + β₃P_t + Σγ_k S_kt + Σδ_j X_jt + ε_t

Where Y_t represents the youth football registration count (or rate per 1,000 youth-age population) in registration period t; T_t is a continuous time variable capturing the pre-existing secular trend; D_t is a binary intervention indicator (0 for pre-December 2022, 1 for post-December 2022) capturing the immediate level change; P_t is a time-since-intervention variable capturing post-intervention slope changes (persistence or decay); S_kt represents seasonal indicator variables (quarter or month fixed effects); X_jt represents control variables including provincial youth population size, economic conditions, and policy change indicators; and ε_t is the error term, modeled with appropriate autocorrelation structure.

The coefficient β₂ captures the immediate post-victory level change — the primary parameter of interest. A statistically significant positive β₂ (p < 0.05) would indicate an immediate registration increase beyond what the pre-existing trajectory would predict. The coefficient β₃ captures whether the post-intervention trend diverges from the pre-intervention trend, distinguishing between a sustained structural shift (β₃ > 0 or β₃ ≈ 0 with elevated level) and a transient spike (β₃ < 0, indicating reversion toward baseline).

For the disaggregated analyses testing H2–H4, the model is estimated separately for each age cohort, province, and gender subgroup, with interaction terms between the intervention indicator and subgroup characteristics used to test for differential effects. Province fixed effects are included in panel specifications to absorb time-invariant provincial heterogeneity.

### Data Requirements and Sources

The ideal dataset for this analysis would comprise monthly or quarterly youth football registration counts from the Argentine Football Association (AFA), disaggregated by province, age cohort (5–8, 9–12, 13–15, 16–18), and gender, spanning at least 2018 through 2024 to provide sufficient pre- and post-intervention observations. Supplementary data from provincial sports ministries and municipal youth leagues would serve as robustness checks and help capture registrations outside the AFA system.

Population denominator data — specifically, provincial youth population counts by age cohort — would be drawn from Argentina's national statistics agency (INDEC) to convert raw registration counts into per-capita rates, controlling for demographic shifts. Provincial economic indicators (GDP per capita, unemployment rates) would be sourced from INDEC and the Ministry of Economy to control for economic confounders that might independently influence registration behavior.

During the proposal stage, several publicly available datasets were identified as potential alternatives or supplements, including football analytics repositories (eddwebster/football_analytics), World Cup match and results datasets (rhit-calviem/Fifa_World_Cup_analysis; julianmancuello/FIFA_World_Cup_2022; Jeffreyjose29/FIFAWorldCupSimulation), and a Messi-specific dataset (juanepstein99/MessiEvolution). However, these datasets primarily contain professional match statistics, player performance data, and tournament results rather than youth registration or grassroots participation data. None of the identified repositories contain AFA youth enrollment records, provincial sports ministry participation data, or municipal youth league registration databases.

### Control Variables and Confounders

The analysis must account for several potential confounders that could produce spurious associations between the World Cup victory and registration changes. Seasonal registration patterns are a primary concern: youth football registrations in Argentina follow a predictable annual cycle tied to the school calendar and the AFA's competitive calendar, with peak enrollment typically occurring in February–March (start of the Southern Hemisphere school year and football season). Quarter or month fixed effects address this concern.

Pre-existing secular trends in youth football participation must also be modeled, as Argentine youth football had been experiencing gradual institutional development prior to 2022, and any observed post-victory increase must be distinguished from a continuation of pre-existing growth. The continuous time variable T_t captures this trend.

Economic conditions represent a significant potential confounder. Argentina experienced severe economic turbulence during the study period, including inflation rates exceeding 90% annually in 2022–2023, which could influence registration behavior through affordability constraints or, conversely, through increased demand for low-cost recreational activities. Lagged provincial economic indicators are included as controls.

Concurrent policy changes — such as AFA youth development initiatives, government sports subsidies, or municipal facility investments — must be identified and controlled for through binary indicator variables. A systematic search of AFA announcements, government press releases, and sports policy databases would be required to identify such confounders.

### Success Criteria

The primary success criterion is a statistically significant positive level-change coefficient (p < 0.05) at the December 2022 intervention point in the aggregate national youth registration series, after adjusting for trend, seasonality, and confounders. The persistence criterion tests whether the slope-change coefficient distinguishes a sustained structural shift from a transient spike. The demographic specificity criterion requires that the effect be significantly larger in the youngest age cohort than in older cohorts. The geographic heterogeneity criterion requires that provinces with lower baseline rates exhibit larger relative increases. The robustustness criterion requires that findings hold across at least two independent data sources.

### Relevance Assessment of Attempted Datasets

Even had the SSL errors been resolved, the attempted datasets would not have been suitable for testing the study's hypothesis. The Capology datasets contain professional football salary, performance, and market value data for European leagues and MLS — they do not contain Argentine youth football registration records. These datasets could potentially inform contextual analyses of professional football labor markets or Messi's career statistics relative to peers, but they cannot address the core research question regarding youth registration rates in Argentina.

The additional datasets identified during the proposal stage — including World Cup match data (rhit-calviem/Fifa_World_Cup_analysis; julianmancuello/FIFA_World_Cup_2022), FIFA simulation data (Jeffreyjose29/FIFAWorldCupSimulation), and Messi career data (juanepstein99/MessiEvolution) — similarly do not contain youth registration or grassroots participation data. These resources could support supplementary analyses of the 2022 tournament's competitive dynamics or Messi's performance trajectory but cannot serve as the dependent variable for the ITS analysis.

### Absence of Required Data

The fundamental data requirement for this study — longitudinal youth football registration records from the AFA, disaggregated by province, age cohort, and gender — was not available through any of the publicly accessible digital repositories searched during the proposal and experiment stages. This absence is consistent with the broader observation in the literature review that no prior study has accessed or analyzed AFA enrollment data for research purposes. The AFA does not appear to publish granular youth registration statistics in machine-readable formats through open data portals, and provincial sports ministry records similarly lack standardized digital publication.

This data gap represents a significant structural limitation not only for the present study but for the broader field of sport participation legacy research in South America. While European and East Asian contexts benefit from relatively well-developed administrative data infrastructure for sport participation monitoring (as evidenced by the data availability supporting studies such as da, 2023, and 正範 & Hahm, 2020), the Argentine and broader South American context lacks comparable data accessibility.

### Provisional Descriptive Context

While the core ITS analysis could not be executed, several contextual observations from the literature can be noted provisionally. Janosov and Szigeti (2023) compiled data on 32 countries, over 800 players, and nearly 300 clubs with estimated market values exceeding 12 billion EUR for the 2022 World Cup, providing structural context for the tournament's competitive environment. The social media analyses by She et al. (2023) and Russo et al. (2024) documented massive public engagement with the tournament, with millions of posts generated globally — a volume that was undoubtedly amplified in Argentina following the victory. Csató (2020) quantified structural inequities in FIFA World Cup qualification, providing institutional context for the tournament system within which Argentina's victory occurred.

These contextual data points confirm the magnitude of the 2022 World Cup as a cultural event but cannot substitute for the registration-level data required to test the participation hypothesis.

## Discussion

### Interpretation of Non-Results

The inability to execute the planned ITS analysis has substantive implications that extend beyond mere technical inconvenience. The absence of publicly accessible youth football registration data in Argentina is itself a finding worthy of discussion, as it reflects broader challenges in sport governance transparency and data infrastructure that are characteristic of many South American football institutions (Orjales, 2020; Bayle, 2024). The fact that no prior study has analyzed AFA enrollment data for research purposes — as confirmed by the comprehensive literature review — suggests that this data infrastructure gap has systematically prevented empirical investigation of participation dynamics that are of significant public interest and policy relevance.

The non-result also highlights a methodological asymmetry in the global sport participation legacy literature. Studies from Europe, East Asia, and North America benefit from relatively robust administrative data systems that enable rigorous quasi-experimental designs (da, 2023; 正範 & Hahm, 2020), while contexts in the Global South — where football participation is often most culturally significant and where the potential impact of sporting icons may be most pronounced — lack the data infrastructure to support equivalent analyses. This asymmetry risks producing a literature that systematically over-represents participation dynamics in data-rich contexts while under-representing those in data-poor but culturally rich environments.

### Theoretical Implications

Despite the absence of empirical results, the theoretical framework developed in this paper contributes to the literature by integrating three previously disconnected domains: mega-event participation legacy research, athlete branding theory, and South American football culture studies. The proposed intensification-of-existing-interest mechanism — which posits that in football-saturated cultures, a World Cup victory may amplify existing participation rather than create new demand — represents a theoretical refinement of the trickle-down effect that has been the subject of sustained skepticism in the legacy literature (Moussi-Beylie, 2025).

The distinction between hosting effects and victory effects, developed in the literature review, is also theoretically significant. The dominant participation legacy literature has focused overwhelmingly on host-nation effects, where the causal pathway involves infrastructure investment, community programming, and sustained domestic media exposure (正範 & Hahm, 2020). Victory effects — where a nation wins a tournament played abroad — operate through fundamentally different mechanisms: emotional identification with the winning team, aspirational modeling by young athletes, and media-driven narrative construction of the victory as a national achievement. The "Podium to Profit" framework of Chankuna (2025) begins to theorize these pathways but requires adaptation for the specific case of youth sport participation in a football-saturated culture.

The demographic specificity hypothesis (H2) — that effects would be concentrated in the youngest age cohort — reflects the insight that registration decisions for children aged 6–12 are primarily made by parents, who may be more susceptible to emotional inspiration from a World Cup victory than adolescents who make their own participation choices. This mechanism connects to the athlete branding literature (Hasaan et al., 2019; Wang, 2024) by suggesting that the brand resonance of a World Cup-winning athlete operates differently across audience segments, with parental decision-makers potentially more responsive to narratives of national pride and achievement than to the athletic performance itself.

### Methodological Lessons

The experience of designing but being unable to execute this study yields several methodological lessons for future research. First, data feasibility assessment must precede research design finalization in contexts where administrative sports data are not systematically published. The proposal stage identified multiple publicly available football datasets, but none contained the specific variables required for the hypothesis test — a mismatch that could have been detected earlier through more thorough data auditing.

Second, the SSL certificate verification failures encountered during the experiment stage highlight the fragility of relying on external data repositories for reproducible research. While the specific technical issue was environmental rather than fundamental, it underscores the importance of data archiving and local caching strategies in computational research pipelines.

Third, the study's experience reinforces the value of mixed-methods designs for investigating athlete influence on youth populations. Ahamed (2025) demonstrated how qualitative semi-structured interviews can complement quantitative data to understand the mechanisms behind participation changes. In the absence of registration data, qualitative approaches — including interviews with youth coaches, club administrators, and parents — could provide valuable evidence regarding the perceived impact of the 2022 World Cup victory on enrollment patterns, even if such evidence cannot support causal claims with the same confidence as an ITS design.

### Policy Implications

The data infrastructure gap identified in this study has direct policy implications. If the AFA and provincial sports ministries seek to understand and leverage the motivational potential of international football success for youth development purposes, they must invest in systematic data collection and publication infrastructure. Without baseline registration data and ongoing monitoring systems, it is impossible to evaluate whether major sporting moments produce participation dividends that could be amplified through targeted policy interventions — such as subsidized enrollment periods, facility expansion in under-served provinces, or coach training programs timed to coincide with peak post-victory enthusiasm.

The theoretical framework developed here also suggests that policy responses to World Cup victories should be differentiated by demographic segment and geographic context. If the intensification-of-existing-interest mechanism is correct, then blanket national campaigns may be less effective than targeted interventions in under-participating provinces and age cohorts where latent demand is most likely to be activated.

### Limitations

This study has several significant limitations. The primary limitation is the complete absence of empirical results due to data unavailability, which means that all hypotheses remain untested. The theoretical framework and methodological design, while grounded in established precedents, have not been validated against Argentine data. The literature review, while comprehensive within the retrieved corpus, may not capture relevant Spanish-language scholarship published in Argentine or Latin American journals that are not indexed in the databases searched. The proposed ITS design, while methodologically rigorous, assumes that registration data exist at sufficient temporal granularity (monthly or quarterly) and geographic disaggregation (provincial level) to support the analysis — an assumption that may not hold even if data access were obtained. Finally, the study does not address the qualitative dimensions of Messi's influence on Argentinian youth — including identity formation, aspiration shaping, and emotional identification — which require ethnographic or interview-based methods beyond the scope of the present quantitative design.

## Conclusion

This paper set out to investigate whether Argentina's 2022 FIFA World Cup victory produced a measurable increase in youth football registration rates, employing an interrupted time series design grounded in the sport mega-event participation legacy literature. The study developed a comprehensive theoretical framework integrating athlete branding theory, mega-event legacy research, and South American football culture scholarship, and specified a rigorous analytical model with clear success criteria and subsidiary hypotheses regarding age specificity, temporal decay, and geographic heterogeneity of the expected effect.

The empirical analysis was not executed due to the unavailability of the required AFA youth registration data through publicly accessible digital repositories, compounded by technical failures in accessing alternative datasets. The hypothesis therefore remains undetermined, and no conclusions can be drawn regarding the existence, magnitude, or characteristics of a "Messi effect" on Argentine youth football participation.

Despite this limitation, the paper makes three contributions to the literature. First, it identifies and documents a significant gap in the sport participation legacy field: the systematic absence of empirical research on superstar athlete effects on youth participation in their home countries, particularly in the Global South. Second, it develops a theoretical framework — the intensification-of-existing-interest mechanism — that refines the trickle-down effect concept for football-saturated cultural contexts. Third, it provides a complete and replicable research design that can be implemented once the requisite data become available, including detailed model specifications, control variable strategies, and success criteria.

The path forward requires institutional investment in sport participation data infrastructure by the AFA, provincial sports ministries, and international bodies such as FIFA and CONMEBOL. The 2022 World Cup victory was a singular cultural moment whose potential impact on youth development deserves rigorous empirical investigation. Without systematic data collection, this investigation will remain aspirational rather than achievable, and the "Messi effect" will persist as a compelling hypothesis rather than an established finding. Future research should also pursue complementary qualitative approaches — including interviews with youth coaches, club administrators, parents, and young players themselves — to capture the experiential dimensions of Messi's influence that quantitative registration data alone cannot reveal.
