SUMMARY OF EXISTING WORK:

The retrieved literature cluster around several broad themes in sport studies — athlete branding and media construction, football governance and geopolitics, youth development systems, and the intersection of sport with national identity — yet remarkably, no paper directly examines Lionel Messi's impact on Argentinian youth. The closest thematic adjacency comes from work on how sporting stars are constructed by media and how their influence operates culturally. Hasaan et al. (2019) propose a framework for understanding the athlete brand life cycle, identifying stages from brand introduction through potential reinvention, which provides a useful lens for thinking about how a figure like Messi accumulates symbolic capital over a career. Mina and Watson (2024) offer a more directly relevant analysis, examining how Colombian media constructed Linda Caicedo's star status through intersecting narratives of race, gender, and class — a methodological template that could be applied to Messi's representation in Argentinian media and its effects on young audiences. Wang (2024) further explores how sports celebrity endorsements function as promotional mechanisms, though this work remains oriented toward commercial outcomes rather than psychosocial or developmental impacts on youth. Rawian et al. (2023) and Avcı and Kilinçarslan (2023) analyze discourse surrounding the FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022, including the narrative framing of football events, which tangentially touches the media ecosystem in which Messi's 2022 World Cup triumph was embedded.

A second thematic cluster addresses football culture, identity, and player development in South America and comparable contexts. Kolsrud (2018) provides a qualitative study of Uruguayan football culture and its role in producing elite players despite a small population, offering a valuable comparative frame for understanding how footballing culture shapes youth aspiration in the Río de la Plata region. Ndubuokwu and Oluwalose (2023) examine grassroots soccer development models in Germany and Spain, contrasting structured academy systems with more fragmented approaches — a comparison that raises questions about how Argentina's own youth development infrastructure may have been influenced by the visibility of Messi's career trajectory. Capon (2012) explores how footballers with migration backgrounds are incorporated into national team narratives and what this means for inclusion and identity, a theme with clear resonance for Messi's own biography as an Argentinian who developed professionally in Europe. Scarfi and Andrade (2022) argue for recentering global history through peripheral nations like Argentina, providing a historiographical framework that situates Argentinian cultural exports — including football stars — within broader patterns of global influence. Orjales (2020) examines the nexus of football, politics, and corruption in Latin America, contextualizing the institutional environment in which Argentinian youth football operates.

A third cluster concerns the strategic and geopolitical uses of sport, including sportswashing, sports diplomacy, and national identity construction. Søyland (2020), Favre (2025), and Louis (2025) each analyze how states deploy sport as an instrument of soft power and identity forging, with Favre specifically addressing Saudi Arabia's use of sport to construct a new national identity. Gift and Miner (2017) argue that political science has systematically understudied the sports-politics nexus, a claim that resonates with the absence of rigorous work on how individual athletes like Messi function as political or cultural symbols within their home nations. Bayle (2024) addresses governance challenges in global sport organisations, while Ginesta et al. (2018) and Arakelian et al. (2019) examine how global football brands interact with local markets and identities. AlAbdullah (2022) studies fan loyalty in Saudi football, and Aydemir (2023) analyzes the semiotics of footballers' body language and emotional expression. Collectively, these works establish that football operates as a dense cultural, political, and economic system — but none isolate the specific mechanism of a single superstar athlete's influence on the aspirations, identity formation, or developmental pathways of youth in his home country.

GAPS:
1. No study in the retrieved literature directly investigates Lionel Messi's impact — whether cultural, psychological, aspirational, or developmental — on Argentinian youth, representing a complete absence of empirical work on what is arguably one of the most significant athlete-youth dynamics in contemporary global sport.
2. While athlete branding frameworks exist (Hasaan et al., 2019; Wang, 2024) and media construction of sporting stardom has been analyzed for other figures (Mina & Watson, 2024), these approaches have not been applied to Messi specifically, nor have they been connected to outcomes among youth audiences such as aspiration formation, sport participation rates, or national identity negotiation.
3. The literature on South American football culture and player development (Kolsrud, 2018; Ndubuokwu & Oluwalose, 2023) focuses on institutional and systemic factors but does not examine how a globally iconic individual athlete might reshape youth perceptions of football as a viable career path or as a vehicle for social mobility within Argentina.
4. There is no research examining whether Messi's 2022 FIFA World Cup victory — extensively covered in media discourse analyses of that tournament (Rawian et al., 2023; Avcı & Kilinçarslan, 2023) — produced measurable shifts in youth football enrollment, national pride, or collective identity among young Argentinians.
5. The intersection of Messi's biography — particularly his childhood departure from Argentina for Barcelona and his complex relationship with the Argentine Football Association — with youth perceptions of national belonging and emigration aspiration remains entirely unexplored, despite relevant frameworks existing in the migration and identity literature (Capon, 2012; Scarfi & Andrade, 2022).
6. No study has employed longitudinal, survey-based, or ethnographic methods to capture how Argentinian youth themselves articulate the meaning and influence of Messi in their lives, leaving the topic dependent on media narratives and secondary commentary rather than primary youth voices.
7. The political and institutional dimensions of Messi's symbolic role in Argentina — including how political actors, football institutions, and educational systems have leveraged or responded to his influence — remain unexamined, despite established frameworks for analyzing the sports-politics nexus in Latin America (Orjales, 2020; Gift & Miner, 2017).
