This approach is directly transferable to classifying training emulation language, career aspiration references, and body comparison commentary as distinct but related content categories in user-generated text about footballers.
- **Topic shift analysis as a proxy for content characterization** (Locatelli et al., 2023): Proposed a computational method for assessing how topics shift in online conversations on social media, using NLP pipelines to detect politicization tones. The methodology of tracking thematic drift in social media discourse could be adapted to detect when conversations about footballers shift from performance discussion toward emulation, aspiration, or body-focused commentary.
- **Statistical player comparison frameworks** (Mazurek, 2018): Developed a statistical approach to quantify player resemblance to Messi using performance metrics, establishing a methodology for systematic cross-player comparison. While focused on on-field performance rather than social media influence, the comparative framework structure is adaptable to comparing social media impact metrics across players.
- **Goal-scoring consistency analysis via CFM exploratory analysis** (Anwar et al., 2026): Compared Ronaldo and Messi across six scoring dimensions using time-duration data and consistency metrics. This Ronaldo-Messi comparative design provides a template for structured cross-player comparison, though the domain is performance statistics rather than social media influence.

- **Ronaldo vs. Messi goal-scoring consistency** (Anwar et al., 2026): Analyzed consistency across six scoring methods (right-foot, left-foot, penalties, free kicks, long-range, headers) using time-between-goals distributions. Found measurable differences in consistency profiles between the two players, establishing that Ronaldo and Messi have statistically distinct performance signatures that could differentially trigger specific types of fan commentary (e.g., headers and physical prowess driving body comparison language).
- **Player resemblance quantification** (Mazurek, 2018): Developed statistical metrics to identify which active players most closely resemble Messi in performance characteristics, demonstrating that quantitative comparison frameworks can differentiate between players on multiple dimensions simultaneously.

- **Bot-generated content diffusion effects** (Ferrara, 2017): Quantified that social bots significantly amplify content diffusion, with bot-generated content achieving measurable differences in spread patterns compared to organic human content. WHAT THIS QUESTION STILL NEEDS:

The retrieved literature reveals a substantial gap at the intersection of sports celebrity influence, social media discourse analysis, and youth behavioral psychology. No existing study in the provided corpus directly examines how specific footballers' social media presence differentially shapes young users' language around training emulation, career aspiration, or body comparison. The available papers provide strong methodological building blocks — multitask NLP classification for content categorization (Ilias & Askounis, 2023), value expression measurement at scale (Epstein et al., 2025), topic shift detection for discourse characterization (Locatelli et al., 2023), and statistical player comparison frameworks (Mazurek, 2018; Anwar et al., 2026) — but none have been applied to the specific question of differential athlete influence on youth social media behavior. The existing football analytics literature (Mazurek, 2018; Anwar et al., 2026; Hewitt & Karakuş, 2023; Li et al., 2024) focuses exclusively on on-field performance metrics and has not been connected to social media influence outcomes.

A new study must accomplish several things that the current literature leaves entirely unaddressed. First, it must construct a multi-platform social media corpus (Instagram, TikTok, Twitter/X, YouTube comments) specifically filtered for young user demographics (ages 13-24) and annotated for three target discourse categories: training emulation language (e.g., "I'm going to train like Ronaldo today"), career aspiration references (e.g., "I want to be the next Mbappé"), and body comparison commentary (e.g., references to physique, musculature, or physical appearance of specific players). Second, it must develop and validate a custom classification pipeline — likely leveraging the multitask learning approach demonstrated by Ilias & Askounis (2023) and the LLM-based value measurement framework of Epstein et al. (2025) — to reliably detect these three discourse types at scale across multiple languages and code-mixed text (Patel et al., 2016). Third, it must implement a controlled comparative design across at least four footballer-centric content streams (Ronaldo, Messi, Mbappé, Haaland), normalizing for follower count, posting frequency, and content type, while filtering out bot-generated and coordinated inauthentic behavior using established detection methods (Ferrara, 2017; Cornelissen et al., 2019; Nizzoli et al., 2020).

Finally, the study must go beyond documenting volume differences to identify the specific content features that drive any observed differential influence. This requires a feature-level analysis examining how variables such as post type (training video, lifestyle image, match highlight, family content), visual composition (shirtless/athletic body display, action shots, casual settings), caption framing (motivational, promotional, personal), and temporal context (off-season vs. match day, tournament periods) moderate the prevalence of emulation, aspiration, and body comparison language. The existing literature on information diets (Kulshrestha et al., 2017) and value expression (Epstein et al., 2025) provides analytical scaffolding, but no prior work has operationalized these concepts in the context of athlete-specific social media influence on youth audiences. The next study must therefore produce both a validated measurement instrument for the three target discourse categories and a multivariate model linking player-level content features to differential youth engagement patterns — filling a gap that is simultaneously methodological (no existing classifier for these categories), empirical (no comparative data across multiple footballers), and theoretical (no account of which content features mediate athlete influence on youth self-concept and behavioral intentions).
