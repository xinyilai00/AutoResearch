RELEVANT METHODOLOGIES:
- [Raman spectroscopy for compositional analysis]: (He & Voronine, 2016) applied Raman spectroscopy to analyze sugar and fat bloom formation in white and dark chocolates, investigating spectroscopic signatures of polymorphic changes across different chocolate phases. While this methodology was used for quality assessment rather than cariogenicity prediction, it demonstrates that spectroscopic techniques can characterize sugar crystallization states and phase transitions in chocolate matrices — properties directly relevant to modeling sugar release kinetics in the oral environment.
- [Rheological characterization of chocolate]: (Bhattacharyya & Joshi, 2022) examined thermal and mechanical rejuvenation effects on chocolate rheology, characterizing solid-liquid transition behavior and time-dependent rheological property evolution under isothermal conditions. This rheological framework — particularly the analysis of how chocolate transitions from solid to molten states under thermal and deformation fields — is methodologically relevant to modeling oral processing and dissolution kinetics of chocolate-based candies, though the study did not address oral conditions specifically.
- [Sugar alcohol substitution analysis]: (Grembecka, 2015) reviewed the physicochemical properties, caloric values, and metabolic profiles of sugar alcohols (polyols) used as sweetener replacements in confections. This review provides comparative data on how different sweetening agents differ in their chemical behavior, which is tangentially relevant to understanding how sugar composition influences cariogenic potential, though no computational modeling of cariogenicity was performed.

RELEVANT DATASETS:
- No directly relevant datasets were identified among the retrieved papers. None of the papers contained datasets on oral retention time, sugar release kinetics, acidogenic pH curves, dental caries incidence by candy type, or computational cariogenicity models. The retrieved literature is overwhelmingly focused on unrelated domains including game theory variants using candy/chocolate metaphors (Cairns, 2015; Mani et al., 2018; Miyadera et al., 2017; Hamilton et al., 2019), distributed computing (Hayes et al., 2008; Hayes et al., 2009; Sanders & Uhl, 2023; Kim, 2010), clinical neurology (Armstrong, 2013; Weil et al., 2018), behavioral economics (Gneezy et al., 2011), and other unrelated fields.
- The rheological data from (Bhattacharyya & Joshi, 2022) on chocolate phase transitions under thermal and mechanical stress could potentially serve as input parameters for a computational model of chocolate dissolution in the oral cavity, but would require substantial adaptation to oral temperature (37°C) and salivary flow conditions.
- The spectroscopic compositional data from (He & Voronine, 2016) on sugar and fat phase distributions in different chocolate types could inform models of sugar availability and release rates, but again was not collected under oral-simulated conditions.

PRIOR QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- [Chocolate rheological transitions]: (Bhattacharyya & Joshi, 2022) demonstrated that chocolate exhibits time-dependent rheological property evolution under isothermal conditions following thermal rejuvenation from a molten state, with behavior described as analogous to physical aging in polymeric glasses. Specific rheological parameters were characterized but under conditions not directly translatable to oral processing (the study focused on industrial-relevant temperature ranges and deformation fields rather than oral conditions).
- [Sugar bloom characterization]: (He & Voronine, 2016) provided Raman spectroscopic evidence distinguishing sugar bloom from fat bloom in white and dark chocolates, identifying distinct spectroscopic signatures for polymorphic changes. No quantitative cariogenicity metrics, sugar release rates, or pH data were reported.
- [Sugar alcohol properties]: (Grembecka, 2015) reported that sugar alcohols are produced by catalytic hydrogenation of carbohydrates and vary in caloric content and glycemic response compared to sucrose. The review noted that these compounds have different metabolic fates, but no quantitative data on oral retention, acidogenic potential, or cariogenicity modeling was provided.

WHAT THIS QUESTION STILL NEEDS:
The retrieved literature reveals a striking and complete gap: no study among the papers examined has addressed the computational modeling of cariogenic or erosive potential across different candy types. There is an absolute absence of research on sugar release kinetics specific to Halloween candy categories (chocolate, gummy/sticky, hard, sour), oral retention time measurements under controlled conditions, or acidogenic pH profile modeling for these confection types. The existing literature on chocolate physical properties (Bhattacharyya & Joshi, 2022; He & Voronine, 2016) provides only tangentially relevant physicochemical characterization conducted under non-oral conditions, and the sweetener review (Grembecka, 2015) addresses substitution chemistry rather than cariogenicity prediction.

A new study must accomplish several things that no retrieved paper has attempted. First, it must develop or adapt a computational framework that integrates candy-specific physical properties (dissolution rate, adhesiveness, viscosity under oral conditions) with established cariogenicity parameters (sugar concentration over time, Stephan curve pH dynamics, critical pH thresholds for enamel demineralization at approximately pH 5.5). Second, it must generate or compile empirical input data on sugar release kinetics and oral retention times for each candy category under simulated or actual oral conditions — data that does not exist in the retrieved literature. Third, it must produce quantitative predictions of cariogenic and erosive potential that allow direct comparison across candy types, ideally yielding a ranked risk profile that could inform public health guidance around Halloween candy distribution.

The methodological gap is substantial: the field needs a validated computational model that bridges food science (candy matrix composition and dissolution physics), oral biology (salivary flow, bacterial fermentation kinetics, plaque pH dynamics), and dental pathology (demineralization thresholds and cumulative exposure models). No such integrated model was found in any of the retrieved papers, and the next study must either construct one de novo or synthesize existing sub-models from the dental caries literature (which was not represented in the retrieved set) into a unified predictive framework.
