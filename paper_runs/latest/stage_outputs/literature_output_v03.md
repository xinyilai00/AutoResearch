SUMMARY OF EXISTING WORK:

The retrieved literature clusters around three broad thematic areas that intersect tangentially with the topic of Ronaldo's impact on teenage athletes, yet none address it directly. The first and most developed cluster concerns **athlete branding and fan engagement**. Scholars have established detailed frameworks for how athlete brands are constructed through media, oral communications, impression management, and social agents (Hasaan et al., 2016; Hasaan et al., 2018), and have demonstrated that athlete brand image attributes—including on-field performance and off-field persona—significantly predict fan commitment, loyalty, and sponsor preference, with self-brand connection serving as a key mediating mechanism (Kunkel et al., 2019; Mahmoudian et al., 2020). Social media platforms, particularly Instagram, have been identified as central to athlete self-branding, with Cristiano Ronaldo explicitly named among the most-followed athletes globally (Basabain et al., 2021; Scelles et al., 2017). Parry (2009) found that footballers are the most commonly cited sporting heroes among young British fans, while Moraes et al. (2019) reviewed the psychological mechanisms through which celebrities influence consumer decision-making more broadly. Related work on the creator economy (Edeling & Wies, 2024) and influencer credibility (Nugroho et al., 2022) further illuminates how digital fame translates into behavioral influence, though these studies focus on consumer purchase intentions rather than athletic development.

A second cluster addresses **social media's influence on adolescent health and body image**. Multiple studies have documented associations between social media exposure and body image dissatisfaction, disordered eating, and unhealthy fitness ideals among young people (Dane & Bhatia, 2023; Suhag & Rauniyar, 2024; Carrotte et al., 2015). Feijóo et al. (2022) specifically examined teenagers' perceptions of influencers' impact on ideal physical appearance and diet, finding that minors often lack the critical literacy to discern persuasive intent in sponsored health content. Liao (2024) addressed short video addiction and its consequences for adolescent social and academic functioning. These studies collectively establish that social media figures—including athletes and fitness influencers—can shape adolescents' health-related beliefs and behaviors, but they do not examine how elite athlete role models specifically influence teenage athletes' training practices, sport-related identity, or career aspirations.

A third, smaller cluster examines **youth athletic development and the lived experiences of young athletes**. Stevenson (1990) explored the early career socialization of international athletes, emphasizing the role of identity formation and "sponsors" who facilitate entry into sport. Williams and MacNamara (2020) investigated the experiences of deselected youth athletes on talent development pathways, revealing the psychological toll of non-progression. Roderick (2006) provided a sociological account of the precarious working lives of professional footballers, countering the glamorous public image of the profession. Uehara et al. (2021) examined how poverty shapes skill development in Brazilian footballers, and Kvillemo et al. (2023) investigated mental health perceptions among elite male soccer players. While these studies provide rich context on the structural and psychological realities of athletic careers, none examine how exposure to celebrity athletes like Ronaldo shapes teenagers' expectations, motivations, or decisions regarding athletic pursuits.

GAPS:
1. No study in the retrieved literature directly investigates the influence of Cristiano Ronaldo—or any single elite footballer—on teenage athletes' training behaviors, sport participation decisions, or athletic identity formation. The athlete branding literature examines fan engagement and consumer outcomes, but does not extend to developmental impacts on adolescent athletes who may aspire to emulate specific players.
2. The intersection of athlete role modeling and adolescent athletic development remains entirely unstudied. While Parry (2009) identified footballers as the predominant sporting heroes among young fans, and Stevenson (1990) examined identity formation in early athletic careers, no research connects these threads to investigate how teenage athletes internalize or act upon the example set by celebrity footballers.
3. The social media and body image literature (Dane & Bhatia, 2023; Suhag & Rauniyar, 2024; Feijóo et al., 2022) has not been applied to the specific context of teenage athletes who follow elite sports figures. It is unknown whether the body image pressures documented among general adolescent populations are amplified, mitigated, or qualitatively different among teenagers who are actively engaged in competitive sport and who compare themselves to idealized athlete physiques promoted on social media.
4. There is a methodological gap: no study employs longitudinal or experimental designs to assess how sustained exposure to a celebrity athlete's social media presence causally influences teenage athletes' self-efficacy, training intensity, career expectations, or mental health outcomes over time. Existing work is predominantly cross-sectional (Kunkel et al., 2019; Mahmoudian et al., 2020; Carrotte et al., 2015) or qualitative with small samples (Basabain et al., 2021; Kvillemo et al., 2023).
5. The potential for celebrity athlete influence to produce both positive motivational effects and harmful unrealistic expectations among teenage athletes has not been investigated. Roderick (2006) and Williams and MacNamara (2020) document the precariousness and high attrition rates of professional football careers, yet no research examines whether teenage athletes who idolize figures like Ronaldo hold calibrated or distorted beliefs about their own probability of reaching elite levels.
6. Cross-cultural and demographic variation in how teenage athletes respond to celebrity athlete influence is unexplored. The literature is geographically fragmented—spanning British (Parry, 2009; Roderick, 2006), Brazilian (Uehara et al., 2021), Saudi (Basabain et al., 2021), and European (Kvillemo et al., 2023) contexts—but no comparative study examines whether Ronaldo's impact on teenage athletes differs across cultures, socioeconomic backgrounds, or gender.
7. The role of mediating factors—such as parental guidance (Soenens et al., 2019), coaching environments (Silva et al., 2023), and peer influence—in shaping how teenage athletes interpret and respond to celebrity athlete content on social media has not been studied. Existing frameworks for athlete brand influence (Hasaan et al., 2018; Kunkel et al., 2019) do not incorporate developmental or ecological moderators specific to adolescent populations.
