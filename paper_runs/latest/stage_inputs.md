## Part 1 Literature Review and Research Gaps
SUMMARY OF EXISTING WORK:

The retrieved literature cluster around several broad themes in sport studies — athlete branding and media construction, football governance and geopolitics, youth development systems, and the intersection of sport with national identity — yet remarkably, no paper directly examines Lionel Messi's impact on Argentinian youth. The closest thematic adjacency comes from work on how sporting stars are constructed by media and how their influence operates culturally. Hasaan et al. (2019) propose a framework for understanding the athlete brand life cycle, identifying stages from brand introduction through potential reinvention, which provides a useful lens for thinking about how a figure like Messi accumulates symbolic capital over a career. Mina and Watson (2024) offer a more directly relevant analysis, examining how Colombian media constructed Linda Caicedo's star status through intersecting narratives of race, gender, and class — a methodological template that could be applied to Messi's representation in Argentinian media and its effects on young audiences. Wang (2024) further explores how sports celebrity endorsements function as promotional mechanisms, though this work remains oriented toward commercial outcomes rather than psychosocial or developmental impacts on youth. Rawian et al. (2023) and Avcı and Kilinçarslan (2023) analyze discourse surrounding the FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022, including the narrative framing of football events, which tangentially touches the media ecosystem in which Messi's 2022 World Cup triumph was embedded.

A second thematic cluster addresses football culture, identity, and player development in South America and comparable contexts. Kolsrud (2018) provides a qualitative study of Uruguayan football culture and its role in producing elite players despite a small population, offering a valuable comparative frame for understanding how footballing culture shapes youth aspiration in the Río de la Plata region. Ndubuokwu and Oluwalose (2023) examine grassroots soccer development models in Germany and Spain, contrasting structured academy systems with more fragmented approaches — a comparison that raises questions about how Argentina's own youth development infrastructure may have been influenced by the visibility of Messi's career trajectory. Capon (2012) explores how footballers with migration backgrounds are incorporated into national team narratives and what this means for inclusion and identity, a theme with clear resonance for Messi's own biography as an Argentinian who developed professionally in Europe. Scarfi and Andrade (2022) argue for recentering global history through peripheral nations like Argentina, providing a historiographical framework that situates Argentinian cultural exports — including football stars — within broader patterns of global influence. Orjales (2020) examines the nexus of football, politics, and corruption in Latin America, contextualizing the institutional environment in which Argentinian youth football operates.

A third cluster concerns the strategic and geopolitical uses of sport, including sportswashing, sports diplomacy, and national identity construction. Søyland (2020), Favre (2025), and Louis (2025) each analyze how states deploy sport as an instrument of soft power and identity forging, with Favre specifically addressing Saudi Arabia's use of sport to construct a new national identity. Gift and Miner (2017) argue that political science has systematically understudied the sports-politics nexus, a claim that resonates with the absence of rigorous work on how individual athletes like Messi function as political or cultural symbols within their home nations. Bayle (2024) addresses governance challenges in global sport organisations, while Ginesta et al. (2018) and Arakelian et al. (2019) examine how global football brands interact with local markets and identities. AlAbdullah (2022) studies fan loyalty in Saudi football, and Aydemir (2023) analyzes the semiotics of footballers' body language and emotional expression. Collectively, these works establish that football operates as a dense cultural, political, and economic system — but none isolate the specific mechanism of a single superstar athlete's influence on the aspirations, identity formation, or developmental pathways of youth in his home country.

GAPS:
1. No study in the retrieved literature directly investigates Lionel Messi's impact — whether cultural, psychological, aspirational, or developmental — on Argentinian youth, representing a complete absence of empirical work on what is arguably one of the most significant athlete-youth dynamics in contemporary global sport.
2. While athlete branding frameworks exist (Hasaan et al., 2019; Wang, 2024) and media construction of sporting stardom has been analyzed for other figures (Mina & Watson, 2024), these approaches have not been applied to Messi specifically, nor have they been connected to outcomes among youth audiences such as aspiration formation, sport participation rates, or national identity negotiation.
3. The literature on South American football culture and player development (Kolsrud, 2018; Ndubuokwu & Oluwalose, 2023) focuses on institutional and systemic factors but does not examine how a globally iconic individual athlete might reshape youth perceptions of football as a viable career path or as a vehicle for social mobility within Argentina.
4. There is no research examining whether Messi's 2022 FIFA World Cup victory — extensively covered in media discourse analyses of that tournament (Rawian et al., 2023; Avcı & Kilinçarslan, 2023) — produced measurable shifts in youth football enrollment, national pride, or collective identity among young Argentinians.
5. The intersection of Messi's biography — particularly his childhood departure from Argentina for Barcelona and his complex relationship with the Argentine Football Association — with youth perceptions of national belonging and emigration aspiration remains entirely unexplored, despite relevant frameworks existing in the migration and identity literature (Capon, 2012; Scarfi & Andrade, 2022).
6. No study has employed longitudinal, survey-based, or ethnographic methods to capture how Argentinian youth themselves articulate the meaning and influence of Messi in their lives, leaving the topic dependent on media narratives and secondary commentary rather than primary youth voices.
7. The political and institutional dimensions of Messi's symbolic role in Argentina — including how political actors, football institutions, and educational systems have leveraged or responded to his influence — remain unexamined, despite established frameworks for analyzing the sports-politics nexus in Latin America (Orjales, 2020; Gift & Miner, 2017).

## Deep Literature Review
RELEVANT METHODOLOGIES:
- **Interrupted Time Series (ITS) regression**: Used by (da, 2023) to estimate the causal impact of the Homegrown Player Rule on Portuguese Futsal's youth and senior national teams. This quasi-experimental design is directly applicable to detecting whether a discrete event (such as a World Cup victory) produced a structural break in youth registration trends over time.
- **Sport mega-event participation legacy analysis**: (正範 & Hahm, 2020) examined the causal connection between the 2002 FIFA World Cup Korea/Japan and subsequent football participation and spectatorship in both host nations, using longitudinal participation data and comparative pre/post event analysis. This is the closest methodological precedent for studying whether a World Cup event drives grassroots football engagement.
- **Multi-criteria systems-based organizational evaluation**: (Sdrolias et al., 2026) applied a systems-based multi-criteria framework to evaluate organizational competitiveness in elite professional football (Greek Super League, 2018–2020), integrating financial, sporting, and governance indicators. While focused on club-level competitiveness rather than grassroots participation, the framework demonstrates how composite indicators can be constructed from heterogeneous institutional data sources.
- **"Podium to Profit" framework**: (Chankuna, 2025) introduced a framework linking international sports victories to macroeconomic and community-level outcomes in Thailand, connecting sports branding, diplomacy, policy alignment, and entrepreneurship enablement with measurable national income contributions. This framework is relevant for theorizing the causal pathway from a sporting triumph to downstream behavioral outcomes such as youth registration.
- **Mega-event sports practice impact evaluation**: (Moussi-Beylie, 2025) systematically reviewed and evaluated the empirical literature on whether major international sporting events (such as the Olympics) actually increase sports practice at the population level, finding that promised participation legacies are frequently overstated and that empirical evidence is mixed. This work provides the critical counter-narrative and methodological benchmarks for assessing participation effects.
- **Qualitative semi-structured interviews for football development**: (Ahamed, 2025) used 18 semi-structured interviews to examine sponsorship barriers and enablers in women's football in Bangladesh, demonstrating how qualitative methods can complement quantitative registration data to understand the mechanisms behind participation changes.

RELEVANT DATASETS:
- **AFA (Argentine Football Association) youth registration records**: Not present in any retrieved paper. No study in the provided corpus uses or references AFA enrollment data, provincial sports ministry records, or municipal youth league registration databases from Argentina. This is a critical data gap.
- **2002 FIFA World Cup participation legacy data (Japan and South Korea)**: (正範 & Hahm, 2020) used longitudinal sport participation and spectatorship data from Japan and South Korea to assess post-World Cup legacy effects. This represents the closest analogue dataset to what would be needed for the Argentine case, though it focuses on host-nation effects rather than victory effects.
- **Portuguese Futsal national team player development data**: (da, 2023) used player-level data from Portuguese Futsal to evaluate the Homegrown Player Rule's impact via Interrupted Time Series and Random Effects Logistic regression, demonstrating the type of institutional registration data that could be analogous to AFA youth enrollment records.
- **Twitter/social media data from 2022 FIFA World Cup**: (She et al., 2023) collected at least 130,000 tweets for sentiment analysis, and (Russo et al., 2024) analyzed approximately 2 million tweets using multilayer network methods to track topic evolution across tournament stages. While not registration data, these datasets could serve as supplementary indicators of public engagement and football interest in Argentina following the 2022 victory.
- **2022 FIFA World Cup player and team network data**: (Janosov & Szigeti, 2023) compiled data on 32 countries, over 800 players, and nearly 300 clubs with estimated market values exceeding 12 billion EUR, providing structural context for the tournament but not downstream participation outcomes.

PRIOR QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- **(正範 & Hahm, 2020)**: Found a causal connection between the 2002 FIFA World Cup and subsequent football participation in Japan and South Korea, though the study focused on host-nation effects (exposure through hosting) rather than victory-driven inspiration. The specific effect sizes and statistical significance levels are not detailed in the abstract, but the study establishes that mega-events can produce measurable participation legacies under certain conditions.
- **(Moussi-Beylie, 2025)**: Found that the empirical literature regularly challenges the promise that major international sporting events produce lasting increases in sports practice, documenting modest and short-lived economic benefits, localized job creation, and inconsistent participation effects. This finding suggests that any Messi-effect on Argentine youth registration may be transient rather than sustained.
- **(da, 2023)**: Applied Interrupted Time Series regression and Random Effects Logistic regression to evaluate the Homegrown Player Rule in Portuguese Futsal, finding a "relatively" limited impact on youth and senior national team effectiveness despite the policy intervention. This demonstrates that even well-intentioned structural interventions in football produce modest measurable effects, setting realistic expectations for the magnitude of any World Cup victory effect.
- **(Chankuna, 2025)**: Established the "Podium to Profit" framework using Germany as a reference case and Thailand as the principal case, linking sports victories to national income through sport branding and policy alignment, though specific quantitative effect sizes for participation outcomes are not reported in the abstract.
- **(Csató, 2020)**: Quantified unfairness in the 2018 FIFA World Cup qualification via Monte Carlo simulations across 102 nations, finding serious differences in qualification probabilities across confederations. While not directly about participation rates, this demonstrates rigorous quantitative methods applied to FIFA-related structural questions.

WHAT THIS QUESTION STILL NEEDS:

The retrieved literature reveals a significant and specific gap: no existing study has examined the effect of a national team's World Cup victory on youth football registration rates in the winning country, let alone in Argentina specifically. The closest precedent is (正範 & Hahm, 2020), which studied participation legacy effects of the 2002 FIFA World Cup, but this examined host-nation effects in Japan and South Korea rather than victory-driven inspiration effects in a winning nation. The distinction is critical — hosting a mega-event involves sustained domestic exposure, infrastructure investment, and media saturation that may operate through entirely different causal mechanisms than a victory achieved abroad. Similarly, (Moussi-Beylie, 2025) provides a comprehensive review of mega-event participation effects but focuses on events like the Olympics and finds that participation legacies are generally overstated, without specifically addressing the unique case of a football-mad nation winning the sport's premier tournament. The question of whether Messi's 2022 victory — a singular cultural moment in a country where football is already the dominant sport — produced a measurable registration bump remains entirely unaddressed.

Methodologically, the next study must employ an Interrupted Time Series design, as demonstrated by (da, 2023) in the Portuguese Futsal context, applied to AFA youth registration data spanning several years before and after December 2022. This design would need to control for seasonal registration patterns, pre-existing growth trends in Argentine youth football, demographic shifts, and potential confounders such as concurrent policy changes or economic conditions. The study should disaggregate data by province, age cohort, and gender to identify whether any effect is concentrated in specific demographics or regions. No retrieved paper has accessed or analyzed AFA enrollment data, provincial sports ministry records, or municipal youth league databases, meaning the proposed study would be the first to bring these data sources to bear on this question. Supplementary analysis using social media sentiment data — as collected by (She et al., 2023) and (Russo et al., 2024) for the 2022 World Cup — could help establish the temporal dynamics of public engagement as a potential mediating variable between the victory and registration behavior.

Finally, the study must confront the theoretical tension identified by (Moussi-Beylie, 2025): the empirical literature suggests that mega-event participation legacies are typically modest and short-lived, yet the Argentine case presents a potentially unique scenario where football is already deeply embedded in national culture and where Messi's victory may have operated more as an intensification of existing participation rather than the creation of new interest. The "Podium to Profit" framework of (Chankuna, 2025) offers a useful theoretical scaffolding for tracing the causal pathway from victory to behavioral outcome, but it must be adapted to the specific institutional context of Argentine youth football governance. The new study should explicitly test whether any observed registration increase is sustained over multiple post-victory seasons or whether it represents a transient spike, as the distinction has significant implications for sports policy and resource allocation by the AFA and provincial authorities.

HYPOTHESIS:
Argentina's victory in the 2022 FIFA World Cup (December 18, 2022) produced a statistically significant, positive structural break in youth football registration rates beginning in the first registration cycle following the victory (Q1 2023), but the effect is concentrated in the 6–12 age cohort, diminishes over subsequent registration cycles, and is more pronounced in provinces with lower baseline registration rates — consistent with an intensification-of-existing-interest mechanism rather than the creation of entirely new participation demand.

EXPERIMENT DESIGN:
The analysis must determine whether a detectable and statistically significant discontinuity in youth football registration trends exists at the point of Argentina's 2022 World Cup victory (December 2022), after controlling for pre-existing secular trends, seasonal registration cycles, demographic shifts, and concurrent economic or policy confounders. It must further characterize the temporal persistence, geographic distribution, and demographic specificity of any observed effect, and adjudicate between two competing interpretations: (a) a sustained structural increase in youth participation driven by victory-inspired engagement, versus (b) a transient spike that reverts to baseline within one to two registration cycles, as the broader mega-event legacy literature would predict (Moussi-Beylie, 2025).

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
- **Primary criterion**: The ITS model identifies a statistically significant positive level-change coefficient (p < 0.05) at the December 2022 intervention point in the aggregate national youth registration series, after adjusting for trend, seasonality, and confounders — indicating an immediate post-victory registration increase beyond what the pre-existing trajectory would predict.
- **Persistence criterion**: The slope-change coefficient in the post-intervention period is tested to distinguish between a sustained structural shift (significant positive slope change persisting through at least four post-intervention quarters) and a transient spike (significant level change followed by a significant negative slope change returning toward baseline within two to three quarters), with the latter outcome being more consistent with the mega-event legacy skepticism documented by (Moussi-Beylie, 2025).
- **Demographic specificity criterion**: The effect is significantly larger in the youngest age cohort (5–8 or 6–12) than in older adolescent cohorts (13–18), supporting the hypothesis that the victory primarily influenced parental enrollment decisions for young children rather than self-initiated participation by older youth.
- **Geographic heterogeneity criterion**: Provinces with lower pre-victory baseline registration rates exhibit larger relative increases, suggesting the victory activated latent demand in under-participating regions rather than merely amplifying already-saturated markets.
- **Robustness criterion**: The finding holds across at least two independent data sources (e.g., AFA registrations and provincial sports ministry records), reducing the likelihood that observed effects are artifacts of a single administrative system's reporting changes.
- **Null result interpretation**: If no significant level or slope change is detected, this constitutes meaningful evidence that even in a football-saturated culture, a World Cup victory does not translate into measurable grassroots participation increases — a finding that directly extends the critical framework of (Moussi-Beylie, 2025) and challenges the "Podium to Profit" causal pathway proposed by (Chankuna, 2025).
